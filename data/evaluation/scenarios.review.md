# HERA Eval — 25 scenarios for review
_25 scenarios · open `data/evaluation/scenarios.json` to edit_

## How to review
For each scenario, ask yourself:
1. **App description plausible?** Could a real developer say this?
2. **Gold-standard risks correct?** No invented regulations? Severities reasonable?
3. **Mitigations actionable?** Could a dev actually implement them?
4. **Coverage right?** Are the `relevant_dimensions` the ones a competent reviewer would cite?

Flag issues in `scenarios_review_notes.md` (which I'll create alongside) — format: `scenario_NN: <what to change>`.

## scenario_01 — mental-health

**AI:** True · **Sensitivity:** high · **Vulnerable pop:** True · **Markets:** EU, CH · **Likely class:** IIa

### App description
> Hi, I'm building a mental-health chatbot called SerenityBot. It uses an LLM (we fine-tuned Mistral-7B) to deliver CBT exercises for users with mild-to-moderate anxiety. Users chat with it daily, log their mood, and the bot adapts its responses based on past sessions. We're targeting young adults (18-30) in France and Switzerland. There's a crisis-keyword detector that pops up a hotline number.

_Persona: Junior product manager at a 12-person startup, technically curious but light on regulation knowledge. Defensive when scope creep into 'medical device' territory is suggested._

**Relevant dimensions (15):** `P1.D1`, `P1.D2`, `P1.D3`, `P1.D4`, `P2.D1`, `P2.D6`, `P3.D2`, `P3.D3`, `P5.D2`, `P5.D4`, `P6.D3`, `P7.D1`, `P7.D2`, `P7.D3`, `P7.D5`

### Expected risks

| Sev | Dim | Risk |
|---|---|---|
| 🔴 high | `P7.D1` | Almost certainly a Software as a Medical Device (SaMD) under MDR — the disclaimer doesn't change the intended purpose. CBT delivery to users with anxiety is a medical intent. |
| 🔴 high | `P7.D3` | Likely high-risk under EU AI Act (Annex III §1 health), requires conformity assessment, post-market monitoring, transparency. |
| 🔴 high | `P1.D1` | No clinical validation of the CBT exercises or the chatbot's adherence to evidence-based protocols. |
| 🔴 high | `P2.D1` | Consent is a single checkbox over a 14-page PDF — not informed consent for processing sensitive health data under GDPR Art. 9. |
| 🟡 medium | `P2.D6` | Young adults can include 18-year-olds with maturing autonomy; chat transcripts are special-category data. |
| 🔴 high | `P1.D4` | Users with anxiety may use the bot as a substitute for professional care; no escalation to a clinician. |
| 🟡 medium | `P3.D2` | Daily engagement loop + mood logging may cultivate dependency or emotional reliance on the bot. |
| 🔴 high | `P5.D2` | Disclaimer is buried; users may not understand the bot is not a therapist. |
| 🔴 high | `P5.D4` | CBT exercises generated by an LLM may not be distinguishable from validated clinical content. |
| 🟡 medium | `P6.D3` | French/English only — Switzerland has German + Italian speakers; no cultural validation of CBT prompts. |

### Expected mitigations

- `P7.D1` — Engage a Notified Body, classify as Class IIa SaMD under MDR Rule 11, prepare a technical file.
- `P7.D3` — Map system to EU AI Act high-risk obligations: risk-management system, data governance docs, human oversight, transparency to users.
- `P1.D1` — Run a single-arm pilot study with 50-100 users + standardized anxiety scale (GAD-7) pre/post, with a clinician on the team.
- `P2.D1` — Layered consent UI: short summary + 'tell me more' expansions; separate consent for crisis-keyword detection and research use.
- `P1.D4` — Mandatory weekly self-assessment; if GAD-7 ≥ 10 for 2 consecutive weeks, surface a 'please consider seeing a professional' nudge.
- `P5.D4` — Visibly tag content as 'AI-generated CBT guidance' vs 'clinically reviewed' modules.

**Expected regulations cited:** MDR 2017/745 Rule 11, EU AI Act Annex III §1(a), GDPR Art. 9, GDPR Art. 7, ISO 82304-2, MDCG 2019-11

_Author notes: Highest-stakes scenario: developer assumes 'disclaimer = not a medical device' (false). Must catch SaMD classification, EU AI Act, and lack of clinical validation. Crisis path is necessary but not sufficient._

## scenario_02 — chronic-condition

**AI:** False · **Sensitivity:** high · **Vulnerable pop:** False · **Markets:** EU, CH · **Likely class:** IIa

### App description
> I'm a senior engineer at MedTech AG. We built GlucoCare, a diabetes management app for Type 1 and Type 2 patients in Switzerland and Germany. Patients log glucose readings from any meter, log carbs, log insulin doses; the app shows trends and exports PDF reports for their endocrinologist. No dose recommendations, no automation. We have a CE-marked Class I device today.

_Persona: Senior software engineer, comfortable with technical detail and regulation. May understate clinical risk because the app 'just logs'._

**Relevant dimensions (12):** `P1.D5`, `P1.D6`, `P2.D1`, `P2.D3`, `P4.D1`, `P4.D2`, `P4.D5`, `P7.D1`, `P7.D2`, `P7.D4`, `P7.D5`, `P7.D7`

### Expected risks

| Sev | Dim | Risk |
|---|---|---|
| 🔴 high | `P7.D1` | MDR Rule 11 likely bumps to Class IIa — software intended to provide info used for decisions with diagnostic or therapeutic purposes (diabetes management informs dosing decisions by the patient). |
| 🟡 medium | `P1.D5` | If glucose readings are imported via OCR or BLE without verification, measurement-source reliability matters more than entry validation. |
| 🟡 medium | `P1.D6` | Patients may rely on trend display to decide insulin doses without consulting physician. |
| 🟢 low | `P4.D2` | PDF export is one-way; lack of FHIR-based exchange limits clinical integration. |
| 🟡 medium | `P4.D5` | Patients with no internet can't sync; if a critical safety update is needed, slow updates delay risk mitigation. |
| 🟢 low | `P7.D4` | EU AWS region is good; but cross-border processing UE/CH needs SCCs since CH adequacy is unilateral. |

### Expected mitigations

- `P7.D1` — Re-classify as IIa under MDR Rule 11, prepare a Notified Body submission, run a clinical evaluation.
- `P1.D6` — Display a prominent banner: 'Do not change insulin doses without consulting your physician.'
- `P4.D2` — Expose FHIR R4 endpoints for Observation (glucose, HbA1c) so EHRs can pull data.
- `P4.D5` — Critical safety updates pushed via FCM/APNS as mandatory, with offline-friendly fallback.

**Expected regulations cited:** MDR 2017/745 Rule 11, MDCG 2019-11, ISO 82304-2, GDPR Art. 9, Swiss nLPD, MedDO/ODim, FHIR R4

_Author notes: Realistic case: mature dev, the right reflex is to confirm MDR re-classification and tighten FHIR interop. Less about consent (already good) more about clinical decision support boundary._

## scenario_03 — ai-clinical

**AI:** True · **Sensitivity:** high · **Vulnerable pop:** False · **Markets:** EU, CH · **Likely class:** IIa-IIb

### App description
> We are launching TriageAI, an LLM-based symptom checker. The user enters symptoms in natural language, the model asks 3-5 follow-ups, then outputs a triage recommendation: self-care, GP appointment, urgent care, or call 112. We use Llama 3.1 70B hosted on our EU servers. Our disclaimer says 'TriageAI is not a medical device, results are informational only.' We target the EU consumer market via App Store and Google Play.

_Persona: Tech founder confident in the model's capabilities, evasive about medical device classification. Quick to cite NHS 111 as precedent._

**Relevant dimensions (17):** `P1.D1`, `P1.D2`, `P1.D3`, `P1.D5`, `P2.D1`, `P2.D2`, `P2.D6`, `P5.D1`, `P5.D2`, `P5.D3`, `P5.D4`, `P6.D1`, `P6.D3`, `P7.D1`, `P7.D2`, `P7.D3`, `P7.D5`

### Expected risks

| Sev | Dim | Risk |
|---|---|---|
| 🔴 high | `P7.D1` | Triage recommendation is intended purpose of diagnostic decision support → SaMD under MDR. 'Informational' label does not change intended purpose (MDCG 2019-11). |
| 🔴 high | `P7.D3` | EU AI Act Annex III §1(a) high-risk: AI in healthcare triage. Conformity assessment + CE marking required. |
| 🔴 high | `P1.D2` | 8% under-triage on chest pain is unacceptable (cardiac MIs missed → death). Requires re-design of high-severity branches. |
| 🔴 high | `P1.D1` | Validation against 3 GPs on 500 cases is far below the evidence bar (NICE ESF tier 3 = comparative study ≥1000 cases or RCT). |
| 🔴 high | `P5.D2` | Two checkboxes do not convey the 8% under-triage rate. |
| 🔴 high | `P6.D1` | Llama trained on US/UK medical data; symptom presentation varies by gender/ethnicity (e.g., MI in women), no fairness audit. |
| 🟡 medium | `P2.D2` | Storing free-text symptoms 12 months is excessive — pseudonymous but reversible via postcode + symptom + age triangulation. |
| 🟡 medium | `P5.D3` | No decision logs visible to user or auditor; cannot explain why a given recommendation was made. |

### Expected mitigations

- `P7.D1` — Submit to Notified Body as Class IIa/IIb SaMD under MDR Rule 11.
- `P1.D2` — Implement a deterministic safety net: any mention of chest pain / SOB / weakness on one side / loss of consciousness → mandatory 'call 112' override regardless of LLM output.
- `P1.D1` — Multi-site prospective cohort study with N≥1000 across EU populations before launch; comparator = standard care.
- `P6.D1` — Sex/ethnicity-stratified evaluation of triage accuracy; document gaps in model card.
- `P2.D2` — Retain symptom logs ≤ 30 days unless user opts in to longer for research; strong pseudonymization (drop postcode for retained logs).

**Expected regulations cited:** MDR 2017/745 Rule 11, MDCG 2019-11, EU AI Act Annex III §1(a), NICE Evidence Standards Framework, GDPR Art. 9, GDPR Art. 25

_Author notes: Classic 'disclaimer doesn't save you' case + AI Act high-risk + under-triage as life-threatening. Must catch all three._

| 🔴 high | `P2.D1` | Cookie banner consent ≠ explicit Art. 9 consent for processing health data for advertising. |
| 🟡 medium | `P2.D2` | Email collection unnecessary for core functionality; ad ID + LMP enables fingerprinting. |

**Expected regulations cited:** GDPR Art. 9, GDPR Art. 22, ePrivacy Directive, MDR 2017/745 Rule 11, MDCG 2019-11

_Author notes: Pregnancy + ads = headline-risk territory. Must catch ad ID + health data combo, miscarriage state, and the borderline SaMD aspect._

## scenario_05 — self-report

**AI:** True · **Sensitivity:** medium · **Vulnerable pop:** False · **Markets:** EU · **Likely class:** Class I or IIa

### App description
> MigraineSense logs your headache episodes plus context like weather, sleep, stress level, food triggers. A small ML model predicts the next likely migraine window over 48h. We use a gradient boosted model trained on 2000 user-years of data. Available in EU. Disclaimer says predictions are 'informational'.

_Persona: Data scientist who built the ML model. Knows ML deeply but light on regulation. Skeptical that prediction = medical device._

**Relevant dimensions (12):** `P1.D1`, `P1.D3`, `P1.D5`, `P2.D1`, `P2.D5`, `P5.D1`, `P5.D2`, `P5.D3`, `P5.D4`, `P6.D5`, `P7.D1`, `P7.D3`

### Expected risks

| Sev | Dim | Risk |
|---|---|---|
| 🔴 high | `P1.D3` | Predictions with 41% recall mean 59% of migraines occur outside predicted windows; users may discount real-world symptoms because the model didn't predict. |
| 🔴 high | `P5.D2` | Precision/recall not communicated to users; they see a confident prediction without uncertainty. |
| 🟡 medium | `P7.D3` | Health-domain AI prediction → check whether high-risk under EU AI Act Annex III §1(a). Likely yes if marketed for clinical use. |
| 🟡 medium | `P7.D1` | If predictions are used to take preventive medication, this becomes a SaMD (clinical decision support). |
| 🟡 medium | `P5.D1` | No explanation of what features drove the prediction — XGBoost feature importances not surfaced. |
| 🟡 medium | `P6.D5` | Training data sample of 2000 user-years — diversity in age, gender, migraine type unclear. |
| 🟡 medium | `P1.D1` | Held-out 20% same-distribution split doesn't generalize; no prospective study. |
| 🟢 low | `P2.D5` | Pseudonymized research data — easily re-identifiable via sleep + location + headache pattern combo. |

### Expected mitigations

- `P1.D3` — Display prediction with confidence + advice: 'Even outside predicted windows, log any symptoms as usual.'
- `P5.D2` — Always show recall/precision in user-friendly terms: 'About 6 in 10 migraines are missed by predictions.'
- `P5.D1` — Show top 3 contributing factors per prediction ('sleep deficit yesterday, weather front coming').
- `P6.D5` — Publish a model card with demographic breakdown of training data; flag underrepresented groups.
- `P7.D1` — Clarify intended purpose: if 'symptom anticipation only' → maybe wellness; if 'inform medication timing' → SaMD path.

**Expected regulations cited:** MDR 2017/745 Rule 11, EU AI Act Annex III §1(a), GDPR Art. 9, MDCG 2019-11

_Author notes: ML prediction case — calibration + uncertainty + false-sense-of-security as the key axis. Easy to miss the recall = 41% problem._

## scenario_06 — adherence

### App description
> RxBuddy helps elderly users (and their caregivers) manage polypharmacy. Caregiver takes photos of prescription labels, an LLM extracts the medication list, dosage, and frequency. A drug-interaction check against the DrugBank database flags potential issues. We target Switzerland and France, currently available in DE/FR/IT/EN.

_Persona: CTO of a digital-health startup, deeply experienced with medication safety. Aware of regulation but trying to minimize burden._

**Relevant dimensions (16):** `P1.D1`, `P1.D2`, `P1.D5`, `P2.D1`, `P2.D6`, `P3.D1`, `P3.D5`, `P4.D3`, `P4.D4`, `P5.D1`, `P5.D4`, `P6.D3`, `P7.D1`, `P7.D2`, `P7.D3`, `P7.D5`

### Expected risks

| Sev | Dim | Risk |
|---|---|---|
| 🔴 high | `P1.D2` | 6% extraction error rate × dangerous combinations = real harm potential; need a hard-stop UI for low-confidence extractions. |
| 🔴 high | `P3.D1` | Caregiver controls the account by default; elderly user's right to make decisions about their own medication can be diluted. |
| 🔴 high | `P3.D5` | Caregivers may follow LLM-flagged interactions without consulting pharmacist (automation bias). |
| 🔴 high | `P7.D3` | GPT-4o is third-party general-purpose AI; under EU AI Act, downstream high-risk integrator obligations apply. |
| 🔴 high | `P2.D6` | Elderly users with cognitive impairment — consent capacity must be assessed. |
| 🟡 medium | `P5.D1` | User must see WHY an interaction is flagged (mechanism, severity, source) not just a warning icon. |

- `P3.D5` — Interaction alerts include 'consult pharmacist' as the only action button for severity ≥ moderate.
- `P7.D3` — Document GPT-4o usage in the technical file with provider's GPAI compliance statement; consider on-prem alternative for resilience.
- `P5.D1` — Interaction detail panel: drug-drug, mechanism, evidence level (DrugBank citation), severity scale 1-5.

**Expected regulations cited:** MDR 2017/745 Rule 11, EU AI Act Annex III §1(a), EU AI Act GPAI obligations, GDPR Art. 9, Swiss MedDO/ODim, ISO 82304-2

_Author notes: Strong dev team that's already doing many things right — the eval is whether the copilot catches the *remaining* nuanced risks (automation bias, GPAI integration, dual-account)._

## scenario_07 — monitoring

**AI:** False · **Sensitivity:** high · **Vulnerable pop:** False · **Markets:** EU, CH · **Likely class:** IIa

### App description
> PulseTrack is the companion app for a CE-marked smartwatch that estimates blood pressure from PPG signals. Users see daily readings + a weekly trend report. It auto-shares with the GP via DEP (Switzerland) or via Sécurité Sociale messaging (France). The watch is Class IIa; the app is the data presentation layer.

_Persona: Mobile developer in a 50-person medical-device company. Trusts the regulatory team's hardware certification, less sure about software-side obligations._

**Relevant dimensions (14):** `P1.D1`, `P1.D5`, `P2.D1`, `P2.D3`, `P2.D4`, `P4.D1`, `P4.D2`, `P5.D1`, `P5.D2`, `P5.D4`, `P6.D5`, `P7.D2`, `P7.D4`, `P7.D5`

### Expected risks

| Sev | Dim | Risk |
|---|---|---|
| 🔴 high | `P1.D5` | PPG BP estimation has known limitations: cold extremities, arrhythmias, motion artefacts. Need to communicate this to users + clinicians. |
| 🔴 high | `P5.D2` | Mean error 4 mmHg can be enough to flip clinical decision (e.g., target 130/80 vs 135/85); users / GPs may not see the confidence interval. |
| 🔴 high | `P5.D4` | Estimated BP must be distinguishable from cuff-measured BP in the GP's interface, especially in DEP. |
| 🟡 medium | `P4.D2` | DEP integration: data format conformance to HL7 CDA / FHIR + Swiss eHealth profile. |
| 🟡 medium | `P6.D5` | N=200 clinical study — was it stratified by skin tone (PPG accuracy differs), age, BMI? |
| 🟢 low | `P2.D4` | Auto-share to GP without granular control (per-reading) is heavy-handed; consider exclusion windows. |

### Expected mitigations

- `P1.D5` — Display known limitations with each reading: 'Tip: this is an estimate. Cold hands, recent exercise, or atrial fibrillation can affect accuracy.'
- `P5.D4` — In the GP report, tag each reading as 'PPG-estimated' with the device + firmware version + confidence interval.
- `P6.D5` — Run a follow-up bias audit by skin tone (Fitzpatrick scale) + age + BMI; publish in model card.
- `P4.D2` — Conform to IHE PCD-01 device-observation profile, Swiss eHealth IPAG profile.

**Expected regulations cited:** MDR 2017/745 Annex II, ISO 81060-2 (BP devices), ISO 82304-2, GDPR Art. 9, Swiss eHealth EPDG/LDEP, HL7 FHIR R4

_Author notes: Mature scenario: regulatory basics are done, the eval tests for finer signals (PPG biases, distinction estimated vs cuff, DEP profile conformance)._

### App description
> DermaScan is a smartphone app that takes a photo of a skin lesion and uses a CNN to classify it as benign, suspicious, or urgent. We market it as a triage aid for primary-care physicians; the GP takes the photo on their phone with informed patient consent. EU + CH. Trained on the ISIC archive.

**Expected regulations cited:** MDR 2017/745 Rule 11, EU AI Act Annex III §1(a), MDCG 2019-11, MDCG 2020-1 (clinical evaluation), GDPR Art. 9, ISO 14971 (risk mgmt)
