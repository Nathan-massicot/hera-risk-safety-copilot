The consent and the privacy information have to be genuinely understandable to a 14-year-old, not adult legalese — informed consent for a minor means age-appropriate, plain-language explanation of what the chatbot does, what's recorded, who can see it (including the parent), and crucially that it is not a clinician or a crisis service [P2.D1]. For special-category minors' data you want strict minimisation and clear limits on using their conversations to train models [P2.D6]. The chatbot also needs a safe-handling path for disclosures of self-harm or abuse — but flag that as its own clinical-safety workstream; here I'm scoping the consent and data side.
U: Alright. Give me the compliance starting list to take to legal and the DPO.
A: Compliance starter, prioritised:
1. [P7.D4] Run a DPIA now (GDPR Art. 35) with the DPO — mental-health + minors + AI is a clear mandatory case; let it drive the consent and data design before build.
2. [P3.D4] Per-country digital-consent logic: GDPR Art. 8 default 16, France 15, Spain 14, Belgium 13. For a 14-year-old this means self-consent in Spain, verifiable parental consent elsewhere. Build verifiable parental consent, easy child-initiated withdrawal (Art. 7), a dual child/parent rights model, and a re-consent step at the age threshold.
3. [P2.D6] Treat all of it as special-category data on vulnerable minors (Art. 9): strict minimisation, tight retention, explicit limits on training the chatbot on their conversations.
4. [P2.D1] Age-appropriate, plain-language consent and privacy notices a 14-year-old can actually understand, stating the app is not a clinician or crisis service.
The single thing that most threatens your autumn date is the per-country consent model — resolve that and the DPIA first.
